01

Meaning and Importance of Data Analytics

Data analytics is the systematic process of examining data to discover useful information, patterns and insights that can support decisions.

Simple

What is data?

Data is a collection of raw facts or observations. Examples include marks, names, attendance percentages, prices, clicks and temperatures.

Next step

What is information?

Information is data that has been processed and organized so that it has meaning. A list of marks becomes useful information when we calculate averages or identify the highest mark.

Core idea

What is analytics?

Analytics means examining data using suitable methods to answer questions, discover patterns and support action.

Why is data analytics needed?

  • Organizations generate large amounts of data.
  • Raw data alone does not automatically provide answers.
  • Analytics helps identify patterns, unusual values and trends.
  • Evidence can support planning, monitoring and improvement.

Characteristics

  • Systematic and question-driven
  • Uses data preparation and analysis
  • Can use statistics, visualization and software
  • Produces findings that should be interpreted in context
Everyday life

YouTube and Netflix use viewing information to personalize recommendations; banking systems monitor transactions for unusual activity.

Education

Attendance, assignment scores and exam marks can help teachers identify students who may need support.

Healthcare

Hospitals can analyze patient records, waiting times and resource usage to improve operations.

Important: Analytics can support a decision, but a useful conclusion also depends on data quality, context, assumptions and domain knowledge.
Data analytics lifecycle — basic introduction +

A simplified lifecycle is: define the problem → collect data → clean/prepare → analyze → visualize/communicate → act/evaluate. Real projects may repeat these stages rather than follow a perfectly straight line.

Advantages and challenges +
  • Evidence-based decisions
  • Performance monitoring
  • Pattern and trend discovery
  • Better planning
  • Missing or inaccurate data
  • Privacy and security concerns
  • Bias in data or methods
  • Misinterpretation of results

Student Doubts

Is data analytics only about numbers?

No. Analytics can involve numerical, categorical, text, image, time-based and other data depending on the problem and methods used.

Is data the same as information?

Not exactly. Data are raw facts; information is data processed or organized so that it conveys useful meaning.

Does analytics always mean prediction?

No. Analytics can describe what happened, investigate why it happened, predict possible outcomes, or support action.

REMEMBERData + Analysis + Insight → Better-supported Decision

Broad analytical practice

The process of examining, cleaning, transforming and interpreting data to discover useful information and support decision-making.

Business-focused analytics

The application of analytical methods to business questions such as sales, customers, operations, finance, marketing and performance.

Student Doubts

Is Business Analytics completely separate from Data Analytics?

No. Business analytics can be understood as a domain-focused application of analytics. Organizations may use the terms differently.

Can a hospital use business analytics?

Yes, if the question is business/operational—for example, analyzing costs, staffing or appointment capacity. A patient-outcome study is more directly a healthcare analytics use case.

REMEMBERData Analytics = broad practice · Business Analytics = business-focused application.

Student Doubts

Does a Data Analyst need machine learning?

Not to perform the core analyst role. Strong foundations in data cleaning, SQL, statistics and visualization are more central. ML basics can be useful later.

Who works with databases most directly?

All three can use databases, but data engineers typically focus more heavily on data storage, pipelines and infrastructure.

05

Role of a Data Analyst

A Data Analyst turns a real-world question into evidence that people can understand and use.

Typical responsibilities

  1. Understand the problem
  2. Obtain data from databases, files or APIs
  3. Clean missing, duplicate and erroneous data
  4. Transform data into useful formats
  5. Analyze and calculate relevant statistics
  6. Find patterns and exceptions
  7. Create visualizations and dashboards
  8. Communicate findings and limitations
  9. Support decision-making

Example

Question: Why did monthly sales fall?

The analyst checks sales by product, region and time period, validates data quality, compares relevant groups, visualizes changes and communicates evidence. The final explanation should distinguish what the data shows from assumptions about why it happened.

Student Doubts

Should an analyst immediately make a dashboard?

No. First understand the question and data. A dashboard is useful only when its measures and visuals answer a meaningful need.

Why is cleaning part of analysis?

Incorrect, duplicate or missing values can distort summaries and conclusions. Preparation is therefore an important part of a reliable workflow.

REMEMBERAsk → Collect → Clean → Analyze → Visualize → Explain → Act.

Student Doubts

Which should I learn first: Python or SQL?

Both are useful. For many analyst workflows, basic SQL is valuable for querying data, while Python becomes powerful for cleaning, analysis and automation.

Do analysts need strong mathematics?

They need practical statistical reasoning. Start with descriptive statistics, distributions, interpretation and uncertainty before moving to more advanced methods.

Common misconception: “Data Analytics and Data Science are exactly the same.” They overlap, but the terms commonly describe different scopes. Organizations use the titles and responsibilities differently.

Student Doubts

Can data science include data analytics?

Yes. Data analytics is commonly considered one component or capability within the broader data science landscape.

Is prediction required for data science?

Not every data science project is predictive. Data science can include experimentation, modeling, optimization and other forms of analysis.

08

Statistics

Statistics is the branch of mathematics that collects, organizes, presents, analyzes and interprets data so that we can draw meaningful conclusions.

Core idea: Statistics converts raw data into meaningful information. For marks such as 45, 60, 72, 55 and 68, it can reveal the average, highest and lowest marks, spread, unusual values and how scores are distributed.
Why it matters

Statistics in data analytics

Analytics often works with large datasets. Statistics helps an analyst summarize data, identify patterns, find unusual observations, compare groups, measure relationships and support data-driven decisions.

Example

1,000 student marks

Instead of examining every mark individually, mean, median, standard deviation, quartiles, percentiles and correlation give a quick view of student performance.

Descriptive statistics

Descriptive statistics organizes, summarizes and describes collected data. It answers: What does the data look like?

  • Central tendency: mean, median and mode
  • Dispersion: range, variance and standard deviation
  • Other measures: quartiles, percentiles, skewness, kurtosis, covariance, correlation and z-score

Inferential statistics

Inferential statistics uses a sample to estimate or draw conclusions about a larger population. It answers: What can we conclude?

  • Estimation and confidence intervals
  • Hypothesis testing and statistical significance
  • t-test, chi-square test, ANOVA and regression
ConceptMeaningExample
PopulationThe complete set of people, objects or observations of interest.All 5,000 students in a college.
SampleA smaller group selected from the population for analysis.200 selected students.
DescriptiveDescribes collected data using summaries, tables and graphs.Average mark of a class.
InferentialGeneralizes from a sample to a population.Estimating college-wide satisfaction from 200 responses.

Student Doubts

Why not study the entire population?

A population may be expensive, time-consuming, difficult or impossible to study. A carefully selected sample can provide useful evidence with fewer resources.

Are descriptive and inferential statistics the same?

No. Descriptive statistics summarizes the data collected; inferential statistics uses sample data to make estimates or conclusions about a wider population.

REMEMBERDescriptive = describe what happened · Inferential = conclude what may be true beyond the sample.
09

Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)

EDA is the process of examining, summarizing, visualizing and exploring a dataset before deeper analysis or modeling.

INSPECTrows & columns
→
CHECKtypes & missing values
→
SUMMARIZEstatistics
→
VISUALIZEpatterns
→
QUESTIONnext analysis

What can EDA discover?

  • Patterns and trends
  • Relationships between variables
  • Missing values and duplicate records
  • Outliers, errors and unexpected values
  • Distributions and possible assumptions

Questions to ask first

  • How many rows and columns are present?
  • What are the column names and data types?
  • Are values missing or duplicated?
  • What is typical, unusual or uneven?
  • Are two variables related?
Numerical checks

Count, mean, median, minimum, maximum, range, quartiles and standard deviation.

Visual checks

Bar charts, histograms, box plots, scatter plots and line charts can reveal structure.

Good practice

Investigate unusual values rather than deleting them automatically. Record assumptions and limitations.

In simple words: EDA means exploring the data before trusting a deeper analysis or model. It helps an analyst understand what the dataset can and cannot support.

Student Doubts

Is EDA only about making graphs?

No. EDA combines inspection, data-quality checks, numerical summaries, visualizations and questions about meaning and context.

Should every outlier be removed?

No. An outlier may be a valid rare observation, a measurement error or a data-entry problem. Investigate it first.

REMEMBERExplore first, understand the data, then choose an appropriate analysis.

Student Performance Analytics

Use a small dataset to connect concepts with an actual analytical workflow.

StudentAttendanceAssignmentInternal ExamExam
Anu95%857888
Rahul72%656055
Meera92%908894
Arun60%505548
Average exam mark71.25
Highest exam mark94 · Meera
Attendance below 75%2 · Rahul & Arun

Questions we can ask

  • Who has the highest exam mark?
  • Which students have attendance below 75%?
  • What is the average exam mark?
  • Which student may require academic support based on selected criteria?

Observation → Analysis → Decision

Observation: Arun has 60% attendance and the lowest exam mark (48).

Analysis: The values meet a selected support criterion, but this small dataset alone does not establish why the mark is low.

Decision: A teacher could review Arun’s attendance, learning progress and other context before deciding on support.

Academic caution: This example does not claim correlation or causation. A larger dataset and appropriate statistical analysis would be needed for such conclusions.
